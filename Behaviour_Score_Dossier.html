Two candidate models were produced from the same feature set: a Weight-of-Evidence / Information-Value scorecard (16 variables, logistic regression, PDO-scaled) built for regulatory interpretability, and a LightGBM gradient-boosting model (SMOTE-balanced, isotonic-calibrated) built for maximum discrimination. Both clear the 40% Kolmogorov-Smirnov benchmark on held-out data and show negligible population drift between the development and validation samples. The GBM was selected as the production scoring engine for the risk-management layer - credit-line decrease/freeze cutoffs, pre-emptive collections routing, and SHAP-derived adverse-action reason codes - while the scorecard is documented as the interpretable model recommended for regulatory sign-off.

01

Objective & Data

Per the brief: build a Behaviour Score - a model predicting the probability that an existing, open, not-past-due credit card account defaults going forward.

Two files were supplied: Dev_data_to_be_shared.csv (96,806 accounts with outcome flag bad_flag, a historical portfolio snapshot) and validation_data_to_be_shared.csv (41,792 accounts, same input variables, no outcome). The deliverable is a predicted default probability for every validation account, plus this documentation.

02

Exploratory Analysis

Notebook: eda.ipynb

  • 01

    Class imbalance. 1,372 of 96,806 accounts defaulted (1.42%) - a rare-event problem. Accuracy is meaningless here; discrimination and calibration metrics (§06) drive every evaluation in this document.

  • 02

    Missing data is structural, not random. 1,185 of 1,214 feature columns carry some missingness. bureau_447 / bureau_436 are 100% missing; onus_attribute_43-48 are ~88% missing - most plausibly "no such bureau record" / "no such product feature" rather than data loss, so these were left as their own WoE/tree-split bin rather than imputed away.

  • 03

    56 zero-variance columns (mostly transaction_attribute_* and low-index bureau_*) were dropped before modelling.

  • 04

    No account overlap between dev and validation, and the two feature distributions are consistent with the brief's description of both as random samples of the same portfolio snapshot (confirmed quantitatively in §06 via PSI).

03

Domain Feature Engineering

Notebook: feature_engineering.ipynb · 14 engineered features across the four input groups, each identified by statistical evidence rather than a data dictionary.

03.1 Credit Utilization & Limit Dynamics

onus_attribute_1 carries the signature of a credit limit (round-number currency, 25,000-2,800,000). onus_attribute_2/17/20/23 are mutually correlated (r = 0.85-0.9997), bounded near [-1.1, 1.25] - the signature of a pre-computed utilization ratio at different snapshots. onus_attribute_2 carries the single highest raw correlation with bad_flag (0.108) of all 1,214 features and became internal_utilization. limit_stress_persistence counts how many of the four snapshots sat at or above 80% utilization.

03.2 Transaction Velocity & RFM

664 unlabeled transaction columns were grouped by correlation-distance hierarchical clustering (k=14). Three clusters stood out by behavioural signature: an everyday-spend cluster (369 columns, used by ~22% of accounts per column), a cash-withdrawal-like cluster (17 columns, used by <0.01% of accounts but with the largest per-use value), and a higher-risk-category cluster (17 columns, 2-5x the target-correlation of any other cluster). These became cash_vs_spend_ratio and highrisk_merchant_share. No reliable 30-vs-90-day nested window could be recovered structurally, so recency is approximated by activity breadth rather than a verified day-window ratio.

03.3 Bureau Stress & Tradeline Health

bureau_433 shares the bounded-ratio signature of the on-us utilization columns and became external_utilization; utilization_gap is internal minus external. Among 288 bounded, low-cardinality bureau columns, pairs ten apart are non-decreasing in >99.9% of rows - the same nested-window signature confirmed for enquiries below - and the top 20 by target correlation became the delinquency_flag_count / worst_bureau_status set.

03.4 Inquiry Velocity

bureau_enquiry_1..50 resolves into 5 blocks of 10 (the same enquiry-purpose ordering, repeated), with the value at block b+1 never less than block b in 100% of rows tested - cumulative enquiry counts over five nested, lengthening lookback windows. credit_seeking_intensity = recent-window sum (block 1) ÷ long-window sum (block 5), a direct read on the "sudden liquidity crunch" pattern named in the brief.

04

Model A · WoE / IV Scorecard

Notebook: scorecard.ipynb · the interpretable, regulator-facing model.

  • 01

    Monotonic WoE binning. Every candidate variable (1,172 after dropping degenerate columns) was fit with optbinning's OptimalBinning (default monotonic trend), which also gives missing values their own bin - no imputation needed.

  • 02

    Information Value screening. Kept variables with 0.02 ≤ IV ≤ 0.5: 411 of 1,172 passed. Ten variables - including onus_attribute_2 (IV 0.696) and bureau_452 (IV 0.703) - were deliberately excluded by the 0.5 cap as near-perfect separators, an overfitting/leakage risk rather than a real signal to build a linear model on.

  • 03

    Parsimony pass. 411 variables is not an auditable scorecard. Variables were ranked by IV and greedily kept only if their WoE was not highly correlated (|r| < 0.6) with an already-kept variable, capped at 20; a Logistic Regression was then fit and any variable with a coefficient of the wrong sign (contradicting its own WoE direction) was dropped and the model refit, iterating to a stable, fully negative-coefficient set of 16 variables spanning all four input groups.

  • 04

    PDO scaling. Standard Points-to-Double-Odds transform, anchored at the conventional 600 points / 50:1 odds / 20 PDO.

Points-to-Double-Odds scaling
Factor = PDO / ln(2) = 28.85
Offset = Base_Score − Factor × ln(Base_Odds) = 487.12
Score = Offset + Factor × ln( (1 − P(default)) / P(default) )

Each variable/bin's point contribution is −Factor × coef × WoE plus an even share of the offset, so summing points across the scorecard's 16 variables reproduces this Score exactly - the point of a scorecard being that a reviewer can trace any account's score back to individual bin contributions by hand.

05

Model B · Gradient Boosting Challenger

Notebook: gbm_model.ipynb · the higher-capacity benchmark model, later selected to drive the risk strategy in §07.

05.1 Class imbalance: three approaches compared head-to-head

Identical LightGBM hyperparameters and early stopping (100-round patience on AUC, held-out calibration split) were used across all three, isolating the imbalance-handling technique as the only variable:

ApproachTest AUCNote
Class weight (scale_pos_weight)0.819No resampling; native missing-value handling preserved
ADASYN oversampling0.835Requires median imputation before resampling
SMOTE oversampling ✓0.836Selected - best AUC, comparable to ADASYN

Oversampling beat class weighting by a modest but consistent margin. The trade-off: SMOTE/ADASYN cannot run on missing values, so training data was median-imputed first - a real cost, since §02 found missingness itself carries signal (e.g. "no bureau record"). Class weighting remains the safer no-imputation fallback if that matters more than the AUC gain in a future iteration.

05.2 Probability calibration: Platt vs. Isotonic

Both calibrators were fit on a calibration split the base model never trained on (via sklearn.frozen.FrozenEstimator, so calibration never re-touches the trees):

MethodAUCBrier
Raw (uncalibrated)0.8360.01356
Platt (sigmoid)0.8360.01390
Isotonic ✓0.8340.01351

Calibration cannot change ranking (AUC is invariant under a monotonic remap) - it is judged on Brier score and the reliability diagram alone. Isotonic won; Platt's sigmoid assumption slightly overcorrected on this rare-event target.

Both models clear every benchmark. The GBM discriminates better (higher KS/Gini) and calibrates marginally better on aggregate Brier - but §06.1 shows calibration is uneven across the risk spectrum, which matters more than the aggregate number once decisions are cut by band.

PSI compares the model's held-out dev-test score distribution against the validation population, decile-by-decile. Both models score at ~0.03-0.06% of the <10% "stable" threshold - expected, since dev and validation are both random draws from the same portfolio snapshot, but worth confirming rather than assuming.

06.1 Calibration is not uniform across the risk spectrum

Decile-level Expected/Actual ratios were computed on top of the aggregate Brier score above. The scorecard stays in a tight 0.83-1.49 band across all ten deciles. The GBM's two safest deciles show E/A ratios of 3.4 and 4.8 - not a calibration failure so much as small-sample noise (1-2 actual defaults inside ~3,000-row deciles at that end of the distribution), but worth flagging before the GBM is used for very tight approval cutoffs at the safe end of the score range.

Risk Management Strategy

Notebook: risk_management.ipynb · the GBM model, scored on the validation set, turned into operating policy.

07.1 Risk bands and credit-line cutoffs

Ten bands were cut on the model's dev-test PD distribution (never re-cut on validation, so each band's expected bad rate is a real out-of-sample number, carried across via the PSI finding in §06), then applied to validation:

Expected bad-rate multiplier by band, relative to portfolio average (dev test, out-of-sample)
Band% of portfolioBad rate vs. avgCredit-line actionCollections
1 - 772.6%0.02x - 0.67xbusiness as usual—
89.7%1.15xenhanced monitoringPre-emptive outreach
98.4%2.28xdecrease linePre-emptive outreach
109.3%5.14xfreeze + block cashPre-emptive outreach

Lift. Bands 8-10 are 27.4% of the validation portfolio (11,471 accounts) by volume, but capture 79.3% of all bad accounts in the dev-test benchmark - band 10 alone (9.3% of accounts) captures 47.8%. This is the concrete justification for concentrating action on this slice: collections outreach is cast over the full 27.4% (a cheap, reversible action, worth a wider net), while the costlier credit-line decrease/freeze is reserved for bands 9-10.

07.2 SHAP adverse-action reason codes

shap.TreeExplainer gives exact, fast per-account attributions for the LightGBM model. Reason codes are generated only for the 11,471 accounts receiving an adverse action (bands 8-10) - matching real adverse-action-notice practice, where a reason is owed for a negative decision, not for business-as-usual. Each account's top-3 positive SHAP contributors are mapped to plain-English text built strictly from what §03 and §04 actually established about that feature - never a specific claim (e.g. "personal loan enquiry") the anonymized columns can't support.

08

Key Insights

  • ·

    Utilization dominates. A single raw column (onus_attribute_2) outperforms every other individual feature and anchors the top reason code for over half of all adverse-action accounts - consistent across the scorecard's IV ranking, the GBM's feature importances, and the SHAP reason-code distribution.

  • ·

    The IV>0.5 cap caught something real. Ten variables scored IV between 0.57 and 0.70 - well past "suspiciously predictive." Excluding them (per the brief's own instruction) is what keeps the scorecard a 16-variable model instead of one dominated by one or two near-target-encoding columns.

  • ·

    Oversampling beat class weighting here, reversing the more common expectation for tree ensembles - at the cost of needing imputation, which likely blunts some of the "missingness is informative" signal found in §02.

  • ·

    An aggregate metric can hide a problem. The GBM's overall Brier score looked fine; only the decile-level Expected/Actual table surfaced the miscalibration at the safe end of the score range (§06.1) - exactly where a tight approval cutoff would live.

  • ·

    A 27%/79% lift. Routing the riskiest 27% of accounts to pre-emptive collections captures 79% of the bad accounts the portfolio will otherwise see - the single number that most directly justifies the cost of a proactive outreach program.

09

Limitations & Recommendations

  • 01

    No data dictionary was supplied. Every semantic label in §03-§07 is a statistically-supported working hypothesis, not a confirmed field definition - flag for the data-owning team before any compliance-sensitive use, particularly the adverse-action reason text in §07.2.

  • 02

    PSI in §06 is a single dev-vs-validation snapshot. Production use should track PSI over successive scoring vintages, and add characteristic-level PSI on the scorecard's 16 variables, not just the score-level check done here.

  • 03

    The GBM's calibration weakness at the safe end of the score range (§06.1) should be re-examined with a larger calibration sample before it drives any very tight approval cutoff.

  • 04

    Recommendation: present the WoE/IV scorecard for regulatory sign-off (auditable, monotonic, 16 hand-traceable variables); keep the GBM as the higher-performance engine behind the operational risk-management layer in §07, where its extra discrimination has direct portfolio value and its lower interpretability is less of a constraint.
